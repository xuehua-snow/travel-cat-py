History

For centuries, Seoul served as the capital of successive Joseon dynasties until becoming the official capital of South Korea in 1948. As an enduring symbol of royal heritage, the Seoul City Wall was constructed by order of the founding monarch of the Joseon Dynasty. This defensive structure, featuring eight gates, once shielded the city from invasion. Today, sections of the restored wall form a scenic walking path encircling the historic city perimeter.
Another landmark is Gyeongbokgung Palace, the primary royal palace and one of Seoul's most iconic landmarks.Seoul's folklore abounds with tales of mysterious creatures. Among the most famous are the “dokkaebi,” mischievous and magical spirits renowned for teasing the greedy and rewarding the kind-hearted. Often depicted wielding a spiked club with a playful grin, they symbolize humor and justice.
Another legendary figure is the nine-tailed fox. It can transform into a beautiful woman, often luring humans in traditional tales to consume its heart or liver. In modern interpretations, however, the nine-tailed fox is frequently portrayed as a being yearning for humanity and love.
