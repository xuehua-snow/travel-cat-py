Toronto

History

Toronto has been home to Indigenous peoples for over 12,000 years, serving as a vital gathering and trading center for numerous communities. In 1793, the area was formally established as York Town, later renamed Toronto in 1834. Over centuries, this city has witnessed numerous historical turning points.
Toronto is also home to many captivating urban legends and folklore tales. One widely circulated legend points to a UFO base beneath Lake Ontario, where persistent mysterious lights and unexplained phenomena continue to fuel speculation about extraterrestrial activity. Another tale, rooted in Indigenous lore, is the myth of “Gashendita,” a fire-breathing dragon said to dwell in the depths of Lake Ontario.

Popular Foods

  • Peameal Bacon Sandwich – Toronto’s signature dish
  • Poutine – Fries, cheese curds, and gravy.
  • Butter Chicken Roti, Jamaican Patties, Sushi Pizza – fusion favorites
  • Butter Tarts and Nanaimo Bars – iconic desserts

Population &
Language

  • English is dominant; 95% speak it.
  • 42.5% have a mother tongue other than English or French.
  • Common home languages: Mandarin, Cantonese, Tagalog, Spanish, Tamil.
  • City of Toronto: ~2.83 million (2025 est.)
  • Greater Toronto Area (GTA): ~7.1 million
  • Over 57% are visible minorities; nearly half are foreign-born
